When retail traders look at global equity markets, they often assume that major stock indices move in lockstep. While it is true that a market sell-off in New York usually triggers a red open in London, the **FTSE 100** and **S&P 500** frequently experience prolonged periods of structural decoupling.

Understanding why the FTSE 100 outperforms during inflationary regimes while the S&P 500 leads during tech-driven bull markets is one of the most powerful macro edges available to UK traders.

---

## 1. Structural Sector Composition: Value vs. Growth

The fundamental driver of index decoupling lies in sector allocation:

```
[S&P 500 Sector Allocation]
- Information Tech & AI: ~30-35%
- Financials: ~13%
- Energy: ~4%

[FTSE 100 Sector Allocation]
- Financials & Banking: ~19%
- Energy & Oil Major: ~14%
- Consumer Staples & Mining: ~25%
- Information Tech: < 1.5%
```

### Key Sector Takeaways:
- **Inflation & High Interest Rate Regimes**: Benefitting banks (higher net interest margins) and energy producers, allowing the **FTSE 100** to outperform.
- **Low Interest Rate & Tech Expansion Regimes**: Lower discount rates inflate future cash flow valuations, causing the **S&P 500** to dramatically outpace value-heavy indexes.

---

## 2. The Currency Factor: GBP Foreign Earnings Effect

One of the unique mechanics of the FTSE 100 is its currency sensitivity. Over 75% of revenues earned by FTSE 100 constituents originate outside the United Kingdom in US Dollars, Euros, and Yen.

```
GBP Weakens (e.g., GBP/USD Drops from 1.35 to 1.25)
   │
   ├─► Overseas USD Revenue Converts into MORE British Pounds
   │
   └─► FTSE 100 Listed Company Earnings Rise automatically
```

Consequently, sharp drops in Cable (**GBP/USD**) often coincide with rallies in the FTSE 100 index price, creating an inverse correlation that surprises uninitiated retail traders.

---

## 3. Relative Strength Pairs Strategy (FTSE / SPX Ratio)

Professional traders monitor the ratio between the FTSE 100 and the S&P 500 (`UK100 / SPX500`) to execute relative strength strategies:

1. **Identify Macro Regime Shifts**: When central banks shift from rate cuts to rate hikes, switch bias toward value-heavy FTSE 100.
2. **Execute Spread Trades**: Go long FTSE 100 and short S&P 500 at key ratio support levels to capture valuation convergence while neutralizing overall market directional risk.

---

## Key Takeaways for Index Traders

- **Never trade the FTSE 100 purely based on S&P 500 charts.**
- **Check commodity prices (Crude Oil & Copper) when analyzing the FTSE 100.**
- **Factor in GBP currency movements when evaluating UK equity index momentum.**
